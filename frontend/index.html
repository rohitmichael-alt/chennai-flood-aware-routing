Flood- and congestion-aware dynamic routing

A Chennai road route changes when a flood-mapped edge is blocked.

Stage 1 connects OpenStreetMap roads with a real OpenCity Chennai 2015 historical flood hotspot dataset. The proof of concept maps flood evidence to road segments, changes the road state, recomputes BPR travel-time cost, and reruns Dijkstra.

Factors considered so far are deliberately limited.

Stage 1 proves the smallest chain first. Later stages add richer flood, traffic, and emergency-routing factors without changing the architecture.

Included in Stage 1

  • OSM road geometry, length, and routability.
  • Historical OpenCity Chennai 2015 flood hotspots.
  • Coordinate reference system validation before spatial mapping.
  • Nearest-road tolerance of 150 meters.
  • Controlled blocked-edge assumption for the demonstration.
  • BPR travel-time cost and Dijkstra route comparison.

Not included yet

  • Live flood detection or live traffic feeds.
  • SRTM elevation and drainage/water-body susceptibility.
  • NASA GPM IMERG rainfall integration.
  • SUMO traffic simulation and accident scenarios.
  • Emergency vehicle priority routing.
  • Full evaluation against static Dijkstra.

How to explain it clearly.

“Ma’am, Stage 1 proves that real Chennai flood evidence can be connected to road segments, converted into a road-state and capacity change, and used to make Dijkstra choose a different route. It is a proof of concept, not the final live system.”

The next stage is to strengthen the road graph.

Stage 2 should turn this prototype into a reusable road-network module before rainfall, simulation, and emergency-routing stages are added.
